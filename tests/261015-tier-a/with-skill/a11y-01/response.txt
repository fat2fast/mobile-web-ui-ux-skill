The claim is too broad. [WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum) requires a pointer target of at least 24 x 24 CSS px or an applicable exception. For the spacing exception, center an imaginary 24 CSS px diameter circle on each undersized target. It must not intersect another target or another undersized target's circle. A row of 20 x 20 CSS px targets can meet this exception with at least 4 CSS px between adjacent targets, provided nearby targets above, below, or at the row ends also clear the circles. "Generous spacing" alone does not prove that geometry. [W3C's explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) gives a 20 px target with a 4 px gap as a passing example.

Decision: Keep the compact row if its measured hit areas and spacing meet SC 2.5.8 and it remains usable. Enlarging the clickable areas to at least 24 x 24 CSS px is a simpler, often more comfortable alternative if spacing fails or accidental taps occur. Check the actual clickable area, not merely the visible icon. Preserve the controls' actions, names, keyboard access, focus visibility, and responsive layout.

Checks to run (NOT RUN here): In the rendered page at mobile and desktop widths, measure every control's pointer hit box and its distance to all neighboring targets, including wrapped rows and nearby controls. Apply the 24 px circle test to each undersized target; retest after text enlargement and responsive changes. Try touch and keyboard operation, inspect focus, and check for accidental activation. Record any specific failing target. This criterion alone cannot establish overall WCAG AA conformance.
